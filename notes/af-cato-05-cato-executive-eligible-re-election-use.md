An executive eligible for re-election will use the powers of office to secure his own continuation — distributing patronage, expanding the military, and cultivating popular favor at public expense. The Roman example shows that executive re-eligibility leads to perpetual occupancy and eventually tyranny. Directly contradicts Hamilton's Federalist #72 defense of re-eligibility. Cited in cases and scholarship on executive term limits and the Twenty-Second Amendment.
