The most powerful Anti-Federalist argument. The supremacy clause combined with the necessary and proper clause grants virtually unlimited power to Congress: whatever it deems necessary for enumerated ends becomes supreme law binding on the states. The taxing power, with no named limit, will expand until federal revenue crowds out the states entirely. A standing army has historically been the instrument of tyranny; free states rely on citizen militias. Representation is fatally thin: sixty-five representatives cannot know the interests of three million people across a continent. Only a small republic can maintain the civic virtue republican government requires. Attributed to Robert Yates of New York.
