The prohibitions on state impairment of contracts protect creditors and maintain commercial confidence. The necessary and proper clause is unavoidable: without it, every subsidiary power would require explicit enumeration — a labyrinthine impossibility no legislature could anticipate. The clause grants implied powers appropriate to each enumerated end. If Congress abuses it, the remedy is political — elections and constitutional amendment — not judicial nullification of all implied powers.
