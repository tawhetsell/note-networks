State and federal governments are both agents of the people — not rivals in a zero-sum contest. If the federal government overreaches, states have tools: legislative obstruction, popular resistance, coordinated opposition. The people's attachment to their states provides an institutional counterweight. Madison describes potential state resistance to unconstitutional federal acts as a natural political check — not a formal legal doctrine.
