The judiciary is the least dangerous branch: it holds neither sword nor purse, but only judgment. Its weakness requires independence, not restraint. Courts must have authority to void acts of Congress that contradict the Constitution — not because courts are superior to the legislature, but because the Constitution is the will of the people, which is superior to the will of their representatives. Lifetime tenure during good behavior is essential: without it, judges bend to whoever controls their continuation. Hamilton anticipates Marbury v. Madison by fifteen years.
