The objections to the Constitution are mutually contradictory: critics simultaneously allege too much federal power and too little. Madison surveys the genuine defects of the Articles that critics ignore — no revenue, no army, no commercial policy, no treaty enforcement — and asks how the contradictory objections could simultaneously be satisfied. Any reform serious enough to fix the Articles would face the same objections now leveled at the Constitution.
