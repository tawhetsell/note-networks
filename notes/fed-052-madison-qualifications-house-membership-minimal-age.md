Qualifications for House membership are minimal — age, citizenship, residency — leaving the widest possible field of eligible candidates. Biennial elections strike the right balance: frequent enough to maintain popular accountability, long enough for representatives to acquire knowledge of national affairs. Cited in cases about congressional qualifications including U.S. Term Limits v. Thornton (1995).
