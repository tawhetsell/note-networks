The mortal disease of popular government is faction — citizens united by passion or interest adverse to the rights of others. Its causes are sown in human nature and cannot be removed without destroying liberty. The only cure is to control faction's effects. In a pure democracy a passionate majority rules directly; no cure exists. A republic interposes representatives who refine popular sentiment. The decisive advantage is the extended sphere: in a large republic, more candidates dilute the risk of an unfit majority; diverse interests make coordinated faction harder to assemble. Size, counterintuitively, protects liberty.
