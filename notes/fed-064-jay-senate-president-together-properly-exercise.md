The Senate and president together properly exercise the treaty power. Senators' six-year terms and small chamber size ensure the deliberation, secrecy, and knowledge of foreign affairs that a larger popular body cannot provide. The president adds executive dispatch and accountability. The men selected for the Senate and presidency will be the most distinguished in the nation — their combined judgment provides the best available guarantee against foolish or corrupt treaties.
