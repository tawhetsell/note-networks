The Convention exceeded its formal mandate — amend the Articles — but acted within the spirit of the people's instruction: create an effective national government. Where the letter and spirit of an authorization diverge, spirit controls. The people, not the Confederation Congress, are the ultimate authority; they can ratify whatever frame their delegates propose. This paper is the principal defense of the Convention's authority to propose an entirely new frame of government.
