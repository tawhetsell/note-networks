The federal government will not encroach on state powers because citizens' daily concerns are entirely local: property rights, personal security, family matters. State governments, being closer to the people, will command greater affection and loyalty than a distant federal authority. Federal power over war, commerce, and revenue leaves all ordinary governance to the states. The gravitational pull of local attachment is the structural check that prevents federal absorption of state functions.
