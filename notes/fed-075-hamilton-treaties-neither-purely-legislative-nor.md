Treaties are neither purely legislative nor purely executive — they are contracts between nations requiring executive secrecy and dispatch in negotiation, combined with the Senate's deliberative check on final consent. The two-thirds Senate requirement provides additional protection against hasty or corrupt agreements. Placing the full treaty power in the president alone would create unilateral executive authority over the most consequential national commitments.
