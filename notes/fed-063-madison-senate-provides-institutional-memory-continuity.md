The Senate provides institutional memory and continuity in foreign policy. No foreign nation will ratify treaties with a government that reverses positions every two years. The six-year term creates a body foreign nations can rely upon. Madison defends the Senate's deliberative character against the charge of aristocracy: it represents the people through election, but with enough distance from immediate passion to act wisely.
