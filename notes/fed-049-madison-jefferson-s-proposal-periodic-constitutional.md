Jefferson's proposal — periodic constitutional conventions to correct encroachments — would unsettle government rather than stabilize it. Frequent appeals to the people would undermine judicial precedent, destabilize constitutional meaning, and reduce the Constitution to a political document subject to factional revision. The proper remedy is structural: separated powers that check each other internally, not external appeals to a temporary popular majority.
