The necessary and proper clause is merely declaratory of an implied power already inherent in any legislative grant. The supremacy clause means only that valid federal law overrides conflicting state law — it confers no new substantive power. Alarm about either clause is misplaced: a law not warranted by the enumerated grants will not be supreme; only legitimate exercises of federal power carry supremacy.
