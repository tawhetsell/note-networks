A bill of rights is not merely desirable but essential. The standard Federalist response — enumerated powers mean unenumerated rights are safe — is historically insufficient: governments routinely expand beyond their grants. A bill of rights provides a textual anchor for liberties that would otherwise face gradual erosion. The Federal Farmer directly rebuts Hamilton's Federalist #84 argument and is among the Anti-Federalist writings credited with forcing the addition of the first ten amendments.
