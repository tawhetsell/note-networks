A well-regulated militia requires federal organization and training; leaving militia regulation entirely to states produces an ineffective defense. Hamilton defends the idea of a select, trained militia over the impractical fantasy of arming every citizen for regular military readiness. The standing army is not a threat as long as the militia is properly maintained as a counterweight. Cited extensively in District of Columbia v. Heller (2008) and McDonald v. City of Chicago (2010) on the Second Amendment.
