The Senate's smaller size, longer terms, and staggered elections provide legislative stability. A second chamber corrects hasty House legislation, maintains consistency in law, and prevents the evils that flow from mutable laws — damaged foreign confidence, economic uncertainty, and opportunities for insiders to profit from sudden legal changes.
