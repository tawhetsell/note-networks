A bill of rights is unnecessary and potentially dangerous. The Constitution, as a grant of enumerated powers, is itself a bill of rights — only what is listed is granted. Enumerating rights creates a negative implication: rights not listed may be denied. Liberty of the press depends on public opinion and government character, not parchment declarations. Hamilton dismisses most remaining objections to the Constitution as answered by the document itself.
