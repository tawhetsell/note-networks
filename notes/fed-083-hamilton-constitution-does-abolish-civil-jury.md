The Constitution does not abolish civil jury trial — it simply does not mandate it in all federal cases (as it does for criminal cases in Article III). The great diversity of state practices on civil juries made uniform constitutional specification impossible in 1787. Constitutional silence is not abolition; Congress retains authority to establish jury trial wherever it deems appropriate. Cited in Seventh Amendment civil jury cases.
